How the loop usually runs

Six rounds, one story

Most B2B SaaS companies in Bengaluru run four to six stages for PMM, partnerships and sales roles. Formats vary by company, so ask the recruiter for the exact loop in the first call.

ROUND 01

Recruiter screen

20 to 30 min

Fit, notice period, CTC range, why you. Have a 90 second intro and your numbers ready.

ROUND 02

Hiring manager

45 to 60 min

Your best two or three stories, how you think, what you would do first. Bring STAR stories with numbers.

ROUND 03

Case or take-home

2 to 5 days

A launch plan, partner plan, territory plan or mock pitch, then a 30 to 45 minute presentation.

ROUND 04

Cross-functional panel

45 to 90 min

Sales, product, CS or partner leaders test how you work with them. Ask how they measure you.

ROUND 05

Leadership or founder

30 to 45 min

Judgement, ambition and culture fit. Have a point of view on the company's market.

ROUND 06

Offer and HR

1 to 2 weeks

CTC split, ESOPs, notice buyout, references and background checks. Get everything in writing.

Question bank

30 questions you will actually get

Ten per role, each with what the interviewer is testing and how to answer. Press practise on any question to try it with the coach.

01

Walk me through a launch you owned end to end. What would you change?

What they are testing: Ownership, cross-functional leadership and whether you measure launches by outcomes, not activity.

How to answer: Name the goal metric first (pipeline, adoption, win rate), then the 3 or 4 moves you made with product, sales and marketing, then the result with a number. Close with one honest change you would make next time.

02

Position our product in one sentence for a CFO, then for an engineer.

What they are testing: Messaging range and whether you understand that different buyers care about different value.

How to answer: For the CFO lead with money or risk (cost, audit, revenue). For the engineer lead with the job it removes and how it works. Same product, two outcomes, each under 20 words. Ask which persona owns the budget.

03

How would you build a competitive battlecard against our biggest competitor?

What they are testing: Sales enablement craft and research discipline.

How to answer: Start from lost deals and seller interviews, not the competitor's website. Keep it to one page: when we win, when we lose, three landmines to set, objection handling, and proof points. Say how you would keep it fresh and measure use.

04

Our win rate fell from 25% to 18% in two quarters. Where do you look first?

What they are testing: Diagnostic thinking with data before opinions.

How to answer: Segment the drop: by segment, deal size, region, competitor and stage where deals die. Read call notes for the losses. Then form two hypotheses, such as a new competitor or a pricing gap, and say how you would test each in 30 days.

05

How do you decide which segment or ICP to go after first?

What they are testing: Prioritisation and commercial judgement.

How to answer: Score segments on pain intensity, ability to pay, ease of reach and product fit, using customer data you already have. Pick one beachhead, define how you would prove it in a quarter, and say what you would deliberately ignore.

06

Tell me about a time sales ignored your messaging. What did you do?

What they are testing: Influence without authority and humility.

How to answer: Use STAR. Show that you went to the field, sat in on calls, found out why the messaging did not land, rebuilt it with two top sellers, and measured adoption. The best answers admit what you got wrong.

07

How do you measure PMM impact when you do not own pipeline?

What they are testing: Whether you can tie your work to revenue honestly.

How to answer: Give a small scorecard: influenced pipeline, win rate in target segments, sales cycle length, feature adoption after launch, and content usage by sellers. Explain how you set a baseline before you start.

08

We want to launch a new pricing tier. How would you test it?

What they are testing: Pricing and packaging sense, plus risk management.

How to answer: Start with willingness to pay interviews and win or loss data. Define who the tier is for and what is fenced in or out. Pilot with new deals in one segment, watch conversion, discounting and cannibalisation, then decide.

09

Present a 30-60-90 day plan for this role.

What they are testing: Whether you learn before you change things, and what you will be judged on.

How to answer: 30 days: listen to 10 customers and 5 sellers, audit assets. 60 days: ship one visible win such as a refreshed pitch or battlecard. 90 days: own a metric and a roadmap. One slide per phase.

10

Pick a B2B SaaS product built in India that you admire. What makes its positioning work?

What they are testing: Product taste and whether you study the market you want to work in.

How to answer: Choose a product you have actually used. Explain who it is for, the one problem it owns, how its message differs from global rivals, and one thing you would change. Specifics beat praise.

01

How would you design a partner program for us from zero?

What they are testing: Structure, commercial sense and focus.

How to answer: Start with the motion you need (resell, refer, co-sell or integrate) and the partner type that fits it. Keep tiers simple, define deal registration, margins or referral fees, enablement and joint plans. Name the first five partners and the 90 day goal.

02

When would you choose a channel partner, an alliance or a technology partner?

What they are testing: Whether you understand different partnership models.

How to answer: Channel partners resell or deliver, so pick them when you need reach and services. Alliances (hyperscalers, big SIs) bring co-sell and credibility. Technology partners integrate, which drives retention and new use cases. Tie each to a revenue goal.

03

A partner signed six months ago and has sourced zero deals. What do you do?

What they are testing: Partner activation and honesty about bad fits.

How to answer: Diagnose first: do their sellers know the product, is there an incentive, do they have the right customers? Run a joint account mapping, pick 10 target accounts, set a 60 day plan with named owners. If nothing moves, deprioritise and say so.

04

How do you avoid channel conflict with our direct sales team?

What they are testing: Rules of engagement and internal influence.

How to answer: Clear deal registration with first in wins, defined territories or segments, and seller compensation that does not penalise partner deals. Review conflicts weekly with sales leadership and fix the rule, not just the deal.

05

Which partners would you go after first in India, and why?

What they are testing: Market knowledge and prioritisation.

How to answer: Map partners by the buyer they already reach: SIs and MSSPs for enterprise, distributors for scale, hyperscaler marketplaces for procurement. Pick the two types that fit our ICP and deal size, and explain how each would make money with us.

06

Walk me through a partnership you negotiated. What did you give up?

What they are testing: Negotiation skill and win-win thinking.

How to answer: Set the context, both sides' goals, the terms that mattered (margin, exclusivity, commitments), and the trade you made to get the deal done. End with what the partnership produced.

07

How do you measure partner success?

What they are testing: Whether you think in pipeline, not logos.

How to answer: Partner sourced and influenced pipeline, closed revenue, time to first deal, percent of partners active in a quarter, certified people per partner, and win rate on partner deals versus direct.

08

How does co-selling with a hyperscaler such as AWS, Azure or Google Cloud work?

What they are testing: Cloud ecosystem fluency, often critical for SaaS partnerships roles.

How to answer: Explain marketplace listings, private offers, how cloud sellers are motivated (consumption and quota credit), and the work needed to get their attention: a clear joint value story, referrals in their tools, and wins to point to.

09

Tell me about a partner relationship that went badly.

What they are testing: Self-awareness and how you recover.

How to answer: Pick a real one. Explain the early signal you missed, what you did once you saw it, and how you protected customers and the company. Share the rule you now follow.

10

You have 20 minutes: build the business case for a new partner.

What they are testing: Structured thinking under time pressure, a common case round.

How to answer: Lay out the partner's customers and our overlap, the joint offer, a simple revenue model (deals per quarter times deal size times win rate), the investment needed, and risks. Recommend go or no-go with a pilot.

01

Sell me this pen. Or sell me our product.

What they are testing: Whether you discover before you pitch.

How to answer: Do not start with features. Ask two or three questions first: when did you last use a pen, what for, what went wrong. Then tie one benefit to their answer and ask for the close.

02

Walk me through your numbers last year: quota, attainment and pipeline.

What they are testing: Honesty and command of your own metrics.

How to answer: Have the numbers ready: quota, attainment percent, deal count, average deal size, cycle length and pipeline coverage. If you missed, say why and what you changed. Never inflate, references get checked.

03

How do you run a discovery call?

What they are testing: Discovery depth and a repeatable process.

How to answer: Name a framework such as MEDDICC or SPICED, then show it in action: confirm the pain and its cost, who decides and how, the timeline, and the next step agreed on the call. Give one example question per step.

04

Tell me about a deal you lost and why.

What they are testing: Learning from losses and honesty.

How to answer: Pick a real loss. Say where it went wrong (single threaded, no champion, late to the real buyer), what you learned and how you now run deals differently.

05

A prospect went silent after a great demo. What do you do next?

What they are testing: Persistence with judgement.

How to answer: Reach other people in the account, send something useful such as a short business case, and use a clear yes or no close: 'Should I close the file?' Show you would have set a dated next step before ending the demo.

06

The buyer says your price is too high. How do you respond?

What they are testing: Objection handling and protecting value.

How to answer: Do not discount first. Ask what they are comparing against, restate the cost of the problem, and trade value for value: longer term, smaller scope or faster payment in exchange for any price move.

07

How do you prioritise accounts in a new territory?

What they are testing: Planning and commercial focus.

How to answer: Tier accounts by fit and signals (hiring, funding, tech stack, compliance needs), pick the top 30 for deep work, and run lighter sequences for the rest. Show weekly activity and pipeline targets.

08

Give me a 60 second cold call.

What they are testing: Composure and a crisp opener.

How to answer: Permission based opener, a reason for the call tied to their role, one sharp question about the problem, and a small ask for 20 minutes. Handle the first brush off calmly and keep it human.

09

Why sales, and why here?

What they are testing: Motivation and research on the company.

How to answer: Give a specific reason you like selling (the problem solving, the scoreboard) and a specific reason for this company: its market, product or growth stage. Show you have researched their customers.

10

How do you work with SDRs, sales engineers and partners in a deal?

What they are testing: Team selling and multi-threading.

How to answer: Show a real deal where you briefed the SE on the business pain, gave SDRs clear account feedback, and brought in a partner for credibility or delivery. Mention the result and how credit was shared.

Four questions every Bengaluru loop asks

Tell me about yourself.

Present, past, future in 90 seconds: your role and best result today, two steps that built the skills this role needs, and why this job is the obvious next step.

Why are you leaving your current role?

Stay positive and forward looking: the scope, market or growth you want next. Never criticise your current employer.

What is your notice period?

State it plainly and what is possible, such as 'officially 90 days, release at 45 to 60 is possible'. Ask whether they offer a notice buyout.

What is your expected CTC?

Give a researched range for the role, split by fixed and variable, and ask about ESOPs and joining bonus. Avoid anchoring on your current pay.

Bengaluru specific

Six things to know before you sign

The parts of a Bengaluru job search that generic interview guides skip.

Notice periods are long

60 to 90 days is common in Indian companies, and startups often want you sooner. Know your real notice period, whether early release is possible, and ask about a notice buyout before you accept.

CTC is more than the number

Offers usually split into fixed pay, variable or OTE, joining bonus and ESOPs. Sales roles often carry a large variable share. Compare offers on fixed pay first. See the PMM Salary 2026 guide for India bands.

ESOPs need four answers

Ask for the vesting schedule and cliff, strike price, exercise window after you leave, and whether the company has ever run a buyback.

Know the company type

GCCs and MNCs run structured, competency based loops. Funded startups lean on cases and founder rounds. Indian SaaS scale ups mix both. Tailor your stories to the type.

Ask about office days

Many Bengaluru teams are hybrid. ORR, Whitefield, Koramangala, HSR and Manyata can mean very different commutes, so ask in the first call.

Referrals still win

A referral from someone in the team usually beats a cold application. Find the hiring manager and a peer on LinkedIn, and apply the same day you ask.
