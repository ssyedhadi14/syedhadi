Simple version

How Qums Is Worked Out

1. Pick your date. Every year on the same day, you look at what you have.

2. Add up the leftover. Cash, bank money, investments, unused gold and anything bought from income that you have not used yet.

3. Take away what is not liable. Money you already paid Qums on, inheritance and mahr, plus gifts for followers of Ayatollah Khamenei. Loans and debts are never taken away.

4. Pay one fifth. 20% of what remains is your Qums. Half is Sahm e Imam and half is Sahm e Sadat.

5. Start fresh. What is left after paying is now khums paid. Next year, enter it in step 3 so it is not taxed again.

FAQ

Common Questions

What is Qums (Khums)?

Qums, also written Khums, means one fifth. In Shia Islam it is a yearly obligation of 20% on the surplus of your income, meaning what is left over after your own and your family's reasonable yearly expenses. Its basis is verse 41 of Surah al Anfal.

When do I calculate it?

Once a year, on your Qums date. For a working person this is usually the anniversary of the day they first started earning. You can pick a date and keep it every year.

Do I pay Qums on my house, car or daily items?

No. The home you live in, your car, furniture, clothes and other things you use, bought within reason for your needs, are not counted. Qums looks at savings and unused items, not things in use.

Is there Qums on gifts?

This is one place where maraji differ. According to the rulings of Ayatollah Sistani, gifts are income and are liable if unspent at year end. According to Ayatollah Khamenei, gifts are generally not liable. Pick your marja in the calculator and confirm with his office.

Is there Qums on inheritance or mahr?

Generally no. Mahr (dowry) and normal inheritance are exempt. Profit made later from inherited or khums paid money can be liable, so ask your marja about your case.

I already paid Qums last year. Do I pay again on the same savings?

No. Money you already paid Qums on is not taxed again. Only the new surplus, and any growth when sold, is counted. Enter last year's khums paid savings in step 3 so it is removed.

Who receives Qums?

Half is Sahm e Imam, given to your marja or his authorised representative. Half is Sahm e Sadat, given to poor believing Sayyids, usually with the permission or through the office of your marja.

Do loans and debts reduce my Qums?

Not in this calculator. Whoever you follow, it does not take away any loan or debt, which keeps the result on the safe side. Some rulings allow limited deductions, such as a debt for the current year's living costs (Islamic Laws, Rulings 1803 and 1804, sistani.org). If you think that applies to you, ask your marja's office before you pay.

Is this calculator a fatwa?

No. It is a planning tool built on commonly published rules. Every marja's office answers personal questions. If anything in your situation is unusual, such as business capital, loans, property or first time payment, ask them before you pay.

This calculator follows commonly published Qums rules, including the rulings of Ayatollah Sistani and Ayatollah Khamenei on the points shown. It is a planning aid, not a fatwa. Rulings differ between maraji and on details such as business capital, loans, property and first time payment. Please confirm your Qums with the office or authorised representative of your marja before paying. Further reading: Rules of Khums, sistani.org and Khums, a brief guide, The World Federation.

Guide

How Do You Calculate Khums?

Quick answer

Khums is 20% of your yearly surplus: income left after your own and your family's reasonable expenses for the year, minus money you already paid Khums on. Half is Sahm e Imam and half is Sahm e Sadat. On a surplus of ₹3,00,000, Khums is ₹60,000.

How to use it

  1. Pick your Khums dateUsually the anniversary of the day you first started earning.
  2. Add savings and unused itemsCash, bank balances, investments and items bought but not used this year.
  3. Remove exempt moneyMoney already Khums paid, mahr, inheritance and items in daily use.
  4. See the split20% Khums with Sahm e Imam and Sahm e Sadat worked out, then download the PDF.

The formula

Surplus = savings and unused items − exempt amounts
Khums = surplus × 20%
Sahm e Imam = Khums ÷ 2, Sahm e Sadat = Khums ÷ 2

Worked example

Surplus ₹3,00,000: Khums = ₹60,000. Sahm e Imam ₹30,000 and Sahm e Sadat ₹30,000.

More questions people ask

Is Khums 20 percent?

Yes. Khums means one fifth, so it is 20% of the yearly surplus that is liable.

Is Khums due on salary?

Salary itself is not taxed when spent on reasonable yearly needs. What remains unspent on your Khums date is surplus, and Khums is due on that.

Is Khums due on gold jewellery?

Jewellery worn in reasonable amounts for your status is usually treated as an item in use. Gold held as savings or investment is liable. Confirm with your marja's office.

Can I pay Khums online in India?

Many marja offices and their authorised representatives accept bank transfers. Use only the official channels listed by your marja.
