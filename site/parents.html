In loving memory

Baba and Muma.The fortress, and the heart that kept it warm.

Syed Mohamed Thaqi1959 to 2020
&
Tasneem Fathima1968 to 2026

I lost both my parents within six years. Grief and gratitude now live side by side in me. This page is for them, and for you, so that whoever reads it calls their own parents a little sooner, and hugs them a little longer.

My parents never asked for recognition. They measured a life by duty, by humility and by how much they loved their children. Everything good in me began with them, and I thank Allah for every year I had with them.

Baba

Syed Mohamed Thaqi

1959 to 2020 · 61 years

Baba lived by principle, discipline and devotion to his family. For 36 years he served Southern Railways in Chennai as a Chemical Metallurgist Analyst, Grade I. He treated his job as a trust, and his honesty earned him a respect from colleagues that outlived his working years.

His last two months were spent in hospital. He carried that illness with sabr, without a word of complaint, trusting completely in what Allah had written. He returned to his Lord on 4 October 2020.

  • Integrity
  • Discipline
  • Patience
  • Quiet sacrifice

His integrity is still the compass I check my own decisions against.

His book

The Signs in the Holy Quran

On 15 December 2011, Baba published a book he had compiled: a single reference to all 114 Surahs and the topics of their ayats, the signs.

It grew out of the Quranic Study Circle that met under Jamat-e-Jafari, Chennai, every Sunday from 11 am to 1 pm. Each week one member gave a 45 minute lecture on 10 to 15 ayats, built on established tafsirs. Mirza Diyanath Ali, Mirza Sajjad Ali and Arabic professor Imtiaz Ali Baig helped him complete it, as Akbar Ali Sayeed, the circle's secretary, records in the preface.

If Baba was the fortress, Muma was the heart that kept it warm.

Muma

Tasneem Fathima

1968 to 2026 · 58 years

Muma was a mother of three, a grandmother to three and a mother-in-law to three. She prayed for everyone and wanted the best for every person she met. Where Baba was strength, she was the warmth around it.

For 21 years she lived with a long-term condition and daily medicines, yet she stayed active and kept herself out of hospital beds. On the morning of Sunday, 8 November 2026, she passed away peacefully in her sleep, at home.

  • Prayer
  • Warmth
  • Empathy
  • Reliance on Allah

I still hear her prayers in every good thing that happens to me.

Gratitude

Four blessings I thank Allah for

Grief asks what you lost. Gratitude asks what you were given. These are the gifts I hold on to.

  1. 01

    27 years of her love

    Twenty seven years of Muma's love, her prayers and her quiet wisdom. Not one of them was owed to me.

  2. 02

    Makkah and Madinah, side by side

    In January 2024 we performed Umrah together and prayed in both holy cities. I got to stand beside my mother in the places she had prayed toward her whole life.

  3. 03

    One roof in Bengaluru

    From May 2022 to November 2026 we lived together in Bengaluru. Shared meals, small talk, quiet laughter. Ordinary days I now know were extraordinary.

  4. 04

    She was there for my Nikkah

    In January 2026 Muma witnessed my Nikkah and blessed it with her duas. Ten months later she was gone, but she saw it. Alhamdulillah.

For you, the reader

What can you do for your parents this week?

If your parents are still with you, you are holding something I would give anything to hold again. None of these take long. All of them last.

📞

Call without a reason

Not to ask for anything. Just to hear their voice and let them hear yours.

🍽️

Eat one meal with them

Phones face down. Let them talk as long as they want.

🙏

Say thank you, specifically

Name one sacrifice they made for you. They have waited years to hear it.

🎙️

Record their voice

A story, a recipe, a dua. One day that recording will be priceless.

💊

Know their medicines

Their doctors, their tablets, their check up dates. Carry some of that weight for them.

🕋

Take them where they dream of going

For my mother it was Makkah and Madinah. Ask yours, then plan it, now.

Before it is too late

What should you ask them while you can?

Once they are gone, the questions stay and the answers go with them. Ask these over chai this weekend.

  1. What was your childhood home like?
  2. How did the two of you meet?
  3. What was the hardest year of your life, and how did you get through it?
  4. What do you wish someone had told you at my age?
  5. Which dua do you make for me most often?
  6. What are you proudest of?
  7. Is there anything you still want to do, or see?
Do it now

Send them a message

Pick one, make it yours, and send it before you close this page.

Send on WhatsApp
If they have already left

How do you keep honouring parents who have passed?

Love does not end at the grave. It changes form. This is how I try to keep serving mine.

Make dua, daily

Name them in your prayers every day. It is the one gift that still reaches them.

Continue their good

Keep alive what they built. For Baba, that means sharing his book with everyone who would read it.

Give in their name

Feed someone, support a student, fund a well. Let their names keep doing good.

Be kind to those they loved

Their siblings, their friends, their neighbours. Honour them through the people they cared for.

Live their values

Baba's integrity at work. Muma's warmth with people. Be the proof of how they raised you.

Visit, and tell their stories

Visit where they rest. Tell your children who they were, so they are never just names.

A dua for our parents

Ya Allah,

  • accept Baba and Muma into Your infinite mercy, and forgive their shortcomings.
  • fill their graves with light.
  • grant them the highest ranks of Jannatul Firdaus.
  • reward every sacrifice they made, every prayer they whispered and every kindness they showed.
  • and reunite our family with them in the gardens of peace.

Ameen.

If this page moved you, please say a short prayer for Syed Mohamed Thaqi and Tasneem Fathima, and for your own parents too.
