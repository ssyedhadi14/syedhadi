We built a content matrix with 23 types in it. It worked, and it was a lot. If you handed me a small team and one quarter tomorrow, I would keep five of those formats and say no to the rest without guilt.

## What the 23 actually were

At AccuKnox the matrix had two halves, and the split matters more than the count. You can see the full list on the [GTM engine](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-engine) page.

Thirteen formats drive traffic. Lists, FAQs, steps, interviews, checklists, definitions, pillar pages, infographics, press releases, ultimate guides, trending content, beginner's guides, and tips and best practices.

Ten formats drive AI visibility and conversions. How to guides, landing pages, product pages, best of posts, comparisons, case studies, alternatives, templates, free tools and reviews.

Before the matrix, content was ad hoc. Blog posts shipped when someone had time, webinars had no follow up, and sales rarely used anything marketing produced. So we mapped every type to the buyer journey and picked topics from real sales questions.

## What one quarter looked like

The [content case study](https://syedcreates.com/content-case-study) has the numbers.

- 12 live moments in a single quarter, made of 8 webinars, 3 offline events and an analyst session
- Website sessions up 240% in 90 days
- The matrix fed the AI visibility work that reached 27.72% share of AI answers, covered in the [AEO and SEO case study](https://syedcreates.com/aeo-seo-case-study)

The lesson we wrote down was simple. Content compounds when one calendar drives every channel. Each webinar became posts, articles and sales assets.

One honest caveat. We did not track closed revenue per format, so I am not going to pretend five formats "closed" deals on their own. What follows is my call, based on what the case study says worked. Pillar articles, buyer guides and free tools shipped on a weekly cadence, and every topic came from a real sales question.

## The five I would keep

![The 23 type content matrix narrowed to five formats that sell, fed by one calendar](/img/blog/23-content-types-5-that-sold-1.png)

1. Pillar pages. One deep page per core problem. It earns the search traffic and becomes the source every other format borrows from. If you only write one long piece a month, write this one.
2. How to guides. Persona and industry guides answer the exact question a buyer types at 11 PM. They sit in the conversion half of the matrix for a reason.
3. Comparisons. Buyers compare you anyway. A fair "you vs them" page lets you frame the criteria, and it is the page a rep can send the moment a competitor enters the deal.
4. Free tools. A calculator or scorecard does the selling while you sleep, and it gives the buyer a reason to come back. I still run my own, like the [GTM health check](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-health-check).
5. Case studies. Sales sends proof, not opinions. One case study with real numbers outlives twenty trend posts.

What did I cut? Press releases, infographics and trending content. They can earn attention, but a small team should spend its hours on assets a rep will forward to a buyer.

## Lenny Rachitsky and the two post rule

When Lenny Rachitsky [walked Substack writers through](https://on.substack.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-earned-65000) how his newsletter grew in its early days, one detail stood out. He said two posts drove about half of his total growth, and his regular weekly posts drove the other half. One of the big ones was an eight part series on building a marketplace business.

He also ran a weekly column answering three reader questions, and he used guest posts on newsletters his audience already read. His verdict on that last tactic was blunt: "Guest posts are effective."

Two lessons carry straight over to B2B. A few deep pieces do most of the work, so protect the time to make them. And the best topics come from real questions, which is exactly why we pulled ours from sales calls.

## Emily Kramer treats content as a portfolio

Emily Kramer's [MKT1 content strategy](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/mkt1-content-strategy-part-1) starts with guiding principles, and two of them map well onto a 23 type matrix. The first is to be willing to miss. She treats content as a portfolio where a few bets drive the outsized returns. The second is to avoid random acts of marketing by testing one big new thing at a time.

Her headline rule is "Add value, every time." She also builds layered content with no dead ends, reusing her frameworks across formats so each idea gets more than one life. That is the same move as turning one webinar into posts, articles and sales assets.

## Gong made data the format

Gong built one of the best known content motions in B2B SaaS. Ross Simmonds broke down [Gong's LinkedIn strategy](https://foundationinc.co/lab/gongs-linkedin-strategy) in 2021 and found a few habits worth stealing.

- Data driven posts with practical sales advice, such as mentioning price later in calls
- Blog posts that end with a "follow us on LinkedIn" prompt instead of a demo ask
- Employees who share and amplify each other's posts
- A company page posting about 10 to 15 times a week

At the time of that analysis Gong had 73,154 LinkedIn followers against 20,491 for its rival Chorus. The format was not novel. The proprietary data and the people distributing it were.

## One calendar, five formats

Picking five formats is only half the system. The other half is reuse. In [Content in a box](https://syedcreates.com/content-in-a-box) each piece gets repurposed into five formats, and distribution runs through partners, community and the newsletter.

Here is the loop I would run with a small team.

1. One webinar or live session a month, built on a real sales question.
2. One pillar page or how to guide from the webinar content.
3. One comparison or case study that a rep can send the same week.
4. Founder and employee LinkedIn posts cut from all three.
5. A monthly review of which assets sales actually sent, judged by pipeline influence.

Each piece feeds the next. Nothing is used once and forgotten.

## What to do this week

1. List every content type you published in the last 90 days.
2. Ask two reps which three assets they sent to buyers last month.
3. Keep the formats on both lists and pause one format nobody sends.
4. Pick one sales question and turn it into a webinar plus a pillar page.
5. Put a monthly "what did sales send" review on the calendar.
