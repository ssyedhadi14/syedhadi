Twenty years from now, your salary will have a number on it that looks wild today. Some of that growth is real, and a big chunk of it is just inflation wearing a nice shirt. The decisions that split those two apart happen in the next few years, not in 2046.

I built the [salary growth calculator](https://syedcreates.com/salary-calculator) from the spreadsheet I have used since my first job. Here is the math behind it, and what it says you should do now.

## What raises in India actually look like

Start with the data, not with hope.

- [Aon's 2026 to 27 survey](https://www.aon.com/apac/in-the-press/asia-newsroom/2026/aon-study-finds-salaries-in-india-projected-to-fise-9-1-in-2027-employers-balance-growth-and-cost-pressures), covering more than 1,200 organisations across 45 industries, found an actual average increase of 9% in 2026 and projects 9.1% for 2027. Attrition rose to 18% in 2026.
- The spread by sector is wide. Aon puts NBFCs at a projected 10.1% for 2027, while technology consulting and services sits at 6.6%.
- The [Deloitte India Talent Outlook 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/average-increment-in-india-is-expected-to-be-9-1-percent-in-2026.html) also expects an average increment of 9.1% in 2026, against 9.0% in 2025. It found 14% of employees were promoted in 2025, while only 7% got the top performance rating.

So a 9% average raise is a fair planning number. But averages hide people. Your sector, your rating and whether you move decide where you land.

## Raises compound, switches jump

A yearly raise multiplies last year's salary. That is compounding, and it is slow and steady. A job switch is a step change. It lifts your base once, and every raise after that compounds on the bigger base.

Here is the part most people miss. Timing matters for what you earn along the way. In my illustrative model below, a single 30% switch in year 3 and the same switch in year 15 land on the same 2046 salary. But the early switch earns you about ₹60 lakh more in total over 20 years, because every year in between pays at the higher base.

The lesson is simple. If a switch is coming, earlier usually beats later.

## Inflation takes a quiet cut

A raise only counts if it beats prices. The RBI projected CPI inflation for 2026 27 at 5.0% in its [August 2026 policy statement](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287), then [raised it to 5.2% in October](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/rbi-hikes-repo-rate-to-5-50-percent-october-2026/).

Do the math on a 9% raise with 5.2% inflation. Your ₹12 lakh becomes ₹13.08 lakh, but in today's money that is about ₹12.43 lakh. Your real raise is roughly 3.6%, not 9%.

A 6% raise in a 5.2% inflation year is almost flat. A 4% raise is a pay cut you did not sign up for.

## A worked example with illustrative numbers

These numbers are illustrative. They are not a forecast for you, and they assume RBI's 5.2% projection holds for 20 years, which it will not exactly do.

Start at ₹12 lakh a year in 2026 and look at 2046.

1. Slow path, 7% a year. Salary in 2046 is about ₹46 lakh. In today's money, about ₹17 lakh.
2. Average path, 9% a year. About ₹67 lakh, or about ₹24 lakh in today's money.
3. Average raises plus three 30% switches, in years 3, 7 and 12. About ₹1.14 crore, or about ₹41 lakh in today's money.

The gap between path 1 and path 3 is not luck. It is a few deliberate moves, made early, and then left alone to compound.

Notice what happens to the "crore" too. Path 3 crosses ₹1 crore on paper, but it buys what about ₹41 lakh buys today. That is the number to plan rent, school fees and retirement around.

## What the calculator does

The [salary growth calculator](https://syedcreates.com/salary-calculator) runs six rules.

1. Your in hand salary compounds by your expected increment every year
2. Needs and wants grow at their own rates, so rent can rise faster than groceries and EMIs stay flat
3. Whatever is left, the surplus, gets invested
4. The PPF share of that surplus equals your age by default, and the rest goes to equity
5. Each year's money compounds from that year to the age you check
6. The final corpus is divided by inflation so you also see it in today's money

One tip. The calculator's PPF default is 8%, from the original spreadsheet. The [current PPF rate is 7.1%](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/small-savings-scheme-interest-rates-unchanged-for-q3-fy27-check-ppf-scss-ssy-rates-558892-2026-09-30) for October to December 2026, so change that box before you trust the result. Run it at a 6% raise and at 14% too, and plan around the lower line.

## Where to find the bigger raise

Expenses have a floor, salary does not have a ceiling. That makes earning more the biggest lever you have.

- Benchmark yourself. If you work in product marketing, check the [India PMM salary benchmarks for 2026](https://syedcreates.com/pmm-salary-2026), built from more than 3,300 public salaries on AmbitionBox and PayScale.
- Look at the market every year, even when you are happy. The [growth and PMM job board](https://syedcreates.com/jobs) is updated weekly.
- Ask for the promotion conversation early. Deloitte's data shows promotions run at roughly twice the share of top rated performers, so a top rating is not the only way up.

## Protect the raise from lifestyle creep

A bigger salary tends to pull spending up with it. In the calculator, if your surplus percentage keeps falling year after year, that is the warning sign. Step up your SIP with every raise so your lifestyle does not eat the whole hike, and use the [FIRE calculator](https://syedcreates.com/fire-calculator) to see when that surplus makes work optional.
