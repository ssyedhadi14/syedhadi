Most GTM advice ages like milk. These five ideas did not. Each one comes from something a GTM or growth leader published in 2025 or 2026, and each one maps cleanly onto the messy job of selling security software with a lean team.

## How I picked these

I set myself three rules. The idea had to come from a piece I could open and read, not a screenshot of a screenshot. It had to change a real decision. And it had to survive contact with a security startup, where buyers are sceptical, deals run through partners and budgets are small.

For context, the team I work with ran on a $100K a year marketing budget and still supported $2M in influenced pipeline. You can read the receipts on the [case studies page](https://syedcreates.com/case-studies). So every idea below gets filtered through one question. Would this help a small team punch above its weight?

## April Dunford says AI is not a position

The idea. In a [January 2026 LaunchPod interview with LogRocket](https://launchpod.logrocket.com/april-dunford-killer-question-expose-weak-ai-product-positioning), April Dunford warns against AI washing. Her point is that "We have AI" no longer counts as a strategy. You have to name the specific value your technology unlocks that competitors cannot match.

What it means in practice. Every vendor slide now says AI. Buyers have learned to skim past it. The word stopped carrying meaning the day everyone started using it.

How I would apply it at a security startup:

1. Write the differentiator in one sentence without the letters A and I.
2. Anchor it on something a buyer can verify. In AI security, my anchor would be the line from my short video on securing AI, that it has to enforce at runtime instead of only reporting.
3. Test it on live deals. The lesson from the [PMM case study](https://syedcreates.com/pmm-case-study) still holds. Product marketing pays off when sales repeats the story without help.

## Kyle Poyar wants fewer channels done better

The idea. Kyle Poyar and Maja Voje surveyed 195 software GTM leaders for the [2025 State of B2B GTM report](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-gtm-report). The average company runs about five core channels and tests five and a half more. The primary motion showed no link to growth rate. 53% reported no or limited impact from AI. Among teams that tried them, intimate events had the highest chance of real impact. His summary for next year is blunt: "2026 needs to be the year of ruthless scaling."

What it means in practice. Ten channels at half strength lose to three channels at full strength. The motion you pick matters less than how hard you run it.

How I would apply it. Our own [GTM case study](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-case-study) followed a simple order. Tracking came first, content second and scale third. I would audit every channel against influenced pipeline, keep the top three and move the rest of the budget into them. I would also keep investing in small rooms. This year I hosted a GrowthX GTM Deep Dive and a CNCF community day, both on the [events page](https://syedcreates.com/events).

## Emily Kramer puts accounts over leads

The idea. In [The Shift to Account Driven GTM](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/account-driven-gtm-part-1) from February 2025, Emily Kramer writes, "There's no excuse to wait for all of your prospects to come inbound anymore." Mapping your whole addressable market into the CRM and enriching it is now cheap enough for small teams. Inbound becomes one intent signal among many, not the main source of contacts.

What it means in practice. The unit of work is the account, not the form fill. Marketing, sales, RevOps and customer success all look at the same list.

How I would apply it. Security deals often run through distributors and resellers, so the account list has to include partners too. Our [partner revenue case study](https://syedcreates.com/partner-revenue-case-study) is built on this. Each partner gets named target accounts, and we run weekly joint pipeline calls and monthly business reviews. I would push it one step further:

- One tiered account list shared by marketing, sales and every active partner
- Account intelligence feeding the tiers, the way we used Factors.ai for the website
- Inbound treated as a signal that moves an account up a tier, not as the finish line

## Ethan Smith wants you cited in the answer

The idea. On [Lenny's Podcast in September 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-aeo-ethan-smith), Lenny Rachitsky and Graphite CEO Ethan Smith walked through answer engine optimization. The episode notes say referral traffic from ChatGPT converts far better than Google search traffic. They also say early startups can show up in AI answers much faster than SEO allows, through landing pages, YouTube videos and authentic Reddit comments. Smith also argues that AI generated content does not work well for this.

What it means in practice. Buyers ask ChatGPT and Gemini for a shortlist before they ever talk to sales. If you are missing from the answer, you are missing from the deal.

How I would apply it. This is the one idea where I already have numbers. The [AEO and SEO case study](https://syedcreates.com/aeo-seo-case-study) shows AccuKnox reaching 27.72% AI visibility, mentioned in 12K of 42K tracked answers, and taking the number one Google answer slot for CNAPP vendors. That took four to six months of SEO, SEM and backlink work plus a 23 type content matrix. My next step would follow the episode. Write the answers humans actually give, in the places answer engines read, and skip the AI slop.

## Elena Verna gives the product away

The idea. On [Lenny's Podcast in December 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-new-ai-growth-playbook-for-2026-elena-verna), Elena Verna explained how Lovable rebuilt its growth playbook. Giving the product away for free became its strongest growth lever. The team holds itself to a "minimum lovable product" standard instead of a minimum viable one, and treats activation as a product job, not a growth job.

What it means in practice. Free value is a distribution channel. The product has to earn love on the first use, because that first use is your ad.

How I would apply it. Security buyers will not swipe a card on day one, but they will try things. Our PMM work already used free trials and self paced training to shorten evaluation. The KubeArmor capture the flag at KubeCon India drew 500+ sign ups and 300+ new GitHub stars. I would treat the open source project, free tools and trials as one funnel, and hand activation to the product team with a clear owner and a weekly number.

## The pattern across all five

Put the five next to each other and one theme shows up. Specific beats generic. A specific claim beats "we have AI". A short channel list beats a long one. A named account beats an anonymous lead. A cited answer beats a buried ranking. A free first use beats a paid ad. None of it needs a bigger budget. It needs sharper choices.

## What to do this week

- Rewrite your homepage headline without the word AI and show it to one salesperson
- List every channel you run and cut any that cannot point to pipeline
- Build a top 50 account list and share it with your best partner
- Ask ChatGPT and Gemini ten buyer questions, then check who gets cited with the [AI visibility checker](https://syedcreates.com/ai-visibility-checker)
- Ship one free thing a buyer can use without talking to you
