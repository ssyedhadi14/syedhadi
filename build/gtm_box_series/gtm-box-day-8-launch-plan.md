Most launches get four weeks of panic and one day of attention. Then the team moves on, the webinar recording sits in a folder, and the leads from the booth wait a week for an email that reads like a receipt.

Today we fix that. You will leave with a six week launch calendar, a webinar run sheet and a follow up rule that turns a launch moment into pipeline you can see.

## A launch is a moment plus a calendar

A launch is not the day you press publish. It is a moment you choose, with work stacked before it and after it. The moment gives people a reason to show up. The calendar makes sure they hear about it, attend, and get a next step while they still care.

Emily Kramer's MKT1 team makes the case well in their [launch playbook episode](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/product-launches-episode-11). They split launches into three tiers. Tier 1 is a huge effort, likely one or two a year at any company size. Tier 2 lands about once a quarter for a narrower audience. Tier 3 is mostly updating web pages, enablement and support content. Their sharpest point is that you should compare a launch with the other campaigns competing for the same people and budget, not with other launches. They also recommend bundling several releases into one bigger moment and planning follow up content at 30, 60 and 90 days.

That matches what I saw at AccuKnox. In one quarter our lean team ran 12 live moments, 8 webinars, 3 offline events and an analyst session, while keeping a weekly content cadence. Website sessions grew 240% in 90 days. The full story is in the [content case study](https://syedcreates.com/content-case-study). The lesson was that content compounds when one calendar drives every channel. Each webinar became posts, articles and sales assets.

The other half of the case is attention after the live moment. [ON24's 2025 benchmarks](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250311690144/en) found that 45% of attendees chose to watch webinars on demand, and that teams using automated nurture sequences saw 69% higher on demand attendance. Almost half your audience shows up after the event. If your plan ends at the live session, you planned for half the room.

## The five step build

This is the launch module from [GTM in a box](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-in-a-box), written so one owner can run it with a designer and one subject matter expert.

![A six week launch timeline from T minus 4 weeks to T plus 2 weeks, with the launch moment in the middle and follow up and repurposing after it](/img/blog/gtm-box-day-8-launch-plan-1.png)

### Step 1. Pick the launch moment

Choose one moment per launch. A webinar, a conference talk, a booth, a data report or a product release. Then tier it.

- Tier 1 gets the full six week calendar, paid support and a partner push.
- Tier 2 gets the calendar without paid media.
- Tier 3 gets a page update, a sales note and one social post.

If a release does not justify Tier 2 on its own, bundle it with the next one. One clear story beats three thin announcements.

### Step 2. Run the T minus 4 to T plus 2 calendar

Six weeks, one owner, one shared sheet. The template below has every row. The rule is simple. Every week has a deliverable and a name next to it.

### Step 3. Write the webinar run sheet

The run sheet removes surprises. Who speaks when, who runs the slides, who reads the chat, which poll goes live at which minute and what the call to action is. Do a full rehearsal two days before, with the same tools and the same links.

### Step 4. Follow up on events within 48 hours

Booth leads and webinar attendees go cold fast. Set one rule. Every lead gets a personal follow up within 48 hours, and every hot lead gets a meeting request with two time slots. Sort leads before the event ends into three groups. Hot leads asked for a demo or pricing. Warm leads asked a real question. Everyone else gets the recording and the next event.

Partners count here too. If a partner shared your booth or cohosted the webinar, send them their leads in the same 48 hours, with a suggested next step for each account.

### Step 5. Run the content repurposing chain

One live moment should feed two weeks of content. A 45 minute webinar becomes:

1. The recording, gated for a week, then open
2. A recap blog post with the three best answers
3. Five short clips for LinkedIn
4. One founder or speaker post with a single strong opinion
5. A sales email with the two minute clip that answers the top objection
6. A partner kit version with their logo slot
7. A newsletter section
8. An FAQ block for the product page, built from audience questions
9. A slide or two for the sales deck
10. A short checklist or one pager as a gated follow up

This is how [Content in a box](https://syedcreates.com/content-in-a-box) gets five formats or more out of every piece.

## The template

Template 1, launch calendar

| Week | Goal | Deliverables | Owner | Done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T minus 4 | Lock the story | Launch brief, tier, speakers, title, registration page, tracking links | Marketing lead | ____ |
| T minus 3 | Open registration | Announcement email, 3 social posts, partner invite kit, sales invite note | Marketing and partners | ____ |
| T minus 2 | Fill the room | Reminder email, speaker posts, paid push for Tier 1, personal invites to 30 target accounts | Marketing and sales | ____ |
| T minus 1 | Rehearse | Final deck, run sheet, full rehearsal, lead sorting rules, follow up emails drafted | Speaker and host | ____ |
| T | Launch moment | Live session or event, live social coverage, lead capture | Host | ____ |
| T plus 1 | Follow up | Personal follow up within 48 hours, meetings booked, recording out, recap post, partner leads shared | Sales and marketing | ____ |
| T plus 2 | Repurpose and report | Clips, sales email, FAQ block, newsletter, pipeline report on meetings and opportunities created | Marketing lead | ____ |

Template 2, webinar run sheet for a 45 minute session

| Minute | Segment | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Welcome and housekeeping | Host | Chat prompt, where they are joining from |
| 3 to 8 | The problem, with one real story | Speaker 1 | No product slides yet |
| 8 to 25 | Main content or live demo | Speaker 1 and 2 | Poll at minute 12 on current approach |
| 25 to 30 | Partner or customer view | Guest | Two questions prepared in advance |
| 30 to 40 | Q and A | Host moderates | Chat reader flags buying questions to sales |
| 40 to 43 | Call to action | Host | One offer only, such as an assessment or demo |
| 43 to 45 | Close | Host | Next event date and survey link |

Back office roles for the run sheet are the host, a slide driver, a chat reader and one person from sales watching the attendee list.

## Worked example

Illustrative numbers, not client data.

A security startup plans a Tier 2 webinar. It gets 400 registrations over three weeks. 150 attend live and 110 more watch the recording in the following two weeks. The chat reader flags 18 buying questions during the session.

Sales sends personal follow ups to all 18 within 48 hours, with two slot options each. 9 meetings get booked. The team turns the session into 10 assets over two weeks. Three of the meetings came from people who only watched the recording after a clip landed in their feed. Without the repurposing chain, those three would not exist.

## Common mistakes

- Treating launch day as the finish line
- Picking a date before picking the story
- Sending a generic "thanks for attending" email a week later
- One call to action per slide instead of one per session
- No sales person watching the attendee list live
- Recording sits unused because nobody owns repurposing
- Partners hear about the launch on launch day

## What to do today

1. Pick your next launch moment and give it a tier.
2. Copy the launch calendar and put one name on every row.
3. Write the 48 hour follow up rule into your sales process.
4. Draft the run sheet for your next webinar, including the chat reader role.
5. List the ten repurposed assets and assign each one before the event happens.
