Signing a partner takes one call and a PDF. Getting that partner to bring you a deal takes a program. Most startups do the first part forty times and never build the second.

Today we build the second part. By the end of this issue you will have tiers, deal registration rules and an account mapping sheet, which is everything a partner needs to know before they spend a single hour selling you.

## Why most partner programs stall

The pattern is always the same. A startup signs a batch of partners, adds their logos to a partner page, runs one enablement call and waits. Six months later two deals are registered and nobody can say which partner touched which opportunity. Leadership concludes that partners "do not work for us."

The partners were rarely the problem. The program never told them three things.

1. What do I earn if I bring you a deal?
2. Will you protect my deal if your direct team finds the same account?
3. Which of my customers should I talk to first?

Tiers answer the first question. Deal registration answers the second. Account mapping answers the third. Skip any one and partners quietly go back to selling the vendor that did answer them.

I learned this running partner programs at AccuKnox with distributors, MSSPs and resellers across 7 regions and 40+ partner organisations, covering $35B of annual TAM. You can read the full [partner revenue case study](https://syedcreates.com/partner-revenue-case-study). The lesson that held in every region was simple. Partners bring pipeline when you bring them named accounts and run a steady review cadence. Logo count is not a metric.

## Tier on what partners source

Big programs already work this way. HubSpot restructured its [Solutions Partner Program](https://www.hubspot.com/solutions-partners-tiers-and-benefits-2026) into five tiers from July 15, 2026, and the thresholds are built on sourced points and total points across sourced, assisted and managed business. The top two tiers also require an average gross revenue retention of at least 75% and 80%. In plain words, HubSpot rewards partners who bring and keep customers, not partners who simply show up.

You do not need points or five tiers. You need the same principle at startup size. Rank partners by the pipeline they source and the customers they keep, and make every benefit flow from that rank.

The rest of this issue is the build. Six steps, a copy ready template with all three sheets, a worked example with numbers, and the mistakes that kill partner programs in their first quarter.

## The six step build

This is the same order I run inside [Partner Revenue in a box](https://syedcreates.com/partner-revenue-in-a-box), compressed so one owner can do it in two weeks.

![A four stage loop for a partner program. Map accounts, tier on sourced pipeline, protect with deal registration, review monthly, then cut or promote every quarter](/img/blog/gtm-box-day-7-partner-program-1.png)

### Step 1. Pick one or two partner types

Each type solves a different problem. Pick the one that matches how your buyers already purchase.

- Distributors reach many resellers in a region and handle billing and credit.
- Resellers and VARs own customer relationships and close deals locally.
- MSSPs run your product as part of a managed service.
- System integrators influence vendor choice in large projects.
- Technology partners make you easier to buy next to their platform.

Start with one or two types for the first 90 days. Add more once the first ones produce pipeline.

### Step 2. Write three tiers, no more

Three tiers keep the math simple enough that a partner can explain it to their own boss. Base every promotion on sourced pipeline, certified people and retention. Review tiers every quarter.

### Step 3. Publish deal registration rules

Deal registration is how partners learn to trust you. [ZINFI's guidance](https://www.zinfi.com/blog/how-long-deal-registration-protection-period-saas/) puts typical SaaS protection windows at 90 to 180 days and suggests 120 days as a sensible default. It also recommends starting protection at approval rather than submission, and asking partners to show activity, such as a scheduled demo, within a set window to keep the registration alive. Write your rules down before the first partner deal, not after the first conflict.

### Step 4. Map accounts to pick your first partners

Do not recruit from a list of logos. Recruit from overlap. Take your ICP account list, ask each candidate partner for their customer base or target list, and count the named accounts you share. A partner call that opens with "here are five of your customers we can win together" moves faster than any partner deck.

Screen every candidate with five questions before outreach. Do they sell to our buyer in our region today? Do they carry a complementary product, not a competitor? Do they have technical staff who can run a demo? Have they closed deals our size in the last year? Will one named person own us internally?

### Step 5. Ship a minimum enablement kit

A one page pitch, a short deck written for the partner's buyer, a demo script, battlecards for the two or three competitors they meet most, pricing guidance and the deal registration form. Record one short training and run one live session per quarter.

### Step 6. Run the monthly business review

Weekly, a 20 minute joint pipeline call with your top partners. Monthly, a business review with each active partner. Quarterly, promote, demote or cut. If a deal is not in the shared tracker, it does not exist.

## The template

Copy these three blocks into one sheet with three tabs.

Tab 1, tier table

| Tier | Entry criteria (per quarter) | Partner gets |
|---|---|---|
| Registered | Signed agreement, 1 trained seller | Base margin, deal registration, partner portal, recorded training |
| Select | 2 registered deals or $50K sourced pipeline, 1 certified engineer | Higher margin, shared leads in their region, joint webinar slot |
| Premier | $250K sourced pipeline, 1 closed deal, 2 certified engineers, renewals on track | Top margin, co marketing funds, named partner manager, roadmap previews |

Replace the dollar figures with numbers that match your deal size. A good rule is that Select should be reachable in one quarter by a partner who is actually trying.

Tab 2, deal registration rules

- Required fields: company name, domain, buyer contact, use case, estimated size, expected close quarter
- Approval or rejection within 2 business days, in writing
- Protection starts on approval and lasts 120 days
- Partner shows activity, such as a scheduled demo, within 30 days or the registration lapses
- First approved registration for a domain wins, duplicates checked by domain
- One extension of 60 days with proof of progress
- Direct sales can see every approved registration in the CRM
- Conflicts go to one named person and are settled within 5 business days

Tab 3, account mapping sheet

| Target account | Industry | Region | Partner | Relationship (customer, prospect, none) | Partner contact | Our owner | Next step | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |

Add one summary row per partner at the bottom with overlap count, number of warm relationships and a fit score from 1 to 5.

## Worked example

Illustrative numbers, not client data.

A security startup with a 200 account ICP list shortlists 25 candidate partners. The five screening questions cut that to 12. Account mapping shows overlap ranging from 2 to 31 named accounts. The startup picks the 4 partners with the most warm relationships, not the biggest brands, and walks into each first call with 5 named accounts.

By day 60, those 4 partners register 9 deals. Two partners reach Select. One partner registers nothing and gets a direct conversation at the first quarterly review. The startup now has a pipeline it can trace to partners, and a clear reason to recruit the next 4 in the same way.

## Common mistakes

- Tiering on revenue alone in year one, which no new partner can reach, so everyone sits in the bottom tier and nobody cares
- Benefits that cost partners effort, such as long certification tracks before their first deal
- Deal registration rules that live in someone's head
- Recruiting 30 partners before mapping a single account
- Running the MBR as a slide show instead of a deal by deal review
- Letting your direct reps quietly work registered accounts

1. Write your partner motion in two sentences. The deal you want and what the partner earns.
2. Pick one or two partner types for the next 90 days.
3. Draft the three tier table with numbers that fit your deal size.
4. Publish the deal registration rules to your sales team before you share them with partners.
5. Map accounts for your top 5 candidate partners and book calls with the 2 who show the most overlap.
