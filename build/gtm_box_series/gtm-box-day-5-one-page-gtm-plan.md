Most GTM plans die inside a 40 slide deck that nobody opens after kickoff. The plan that actually runs a quarter fits on one page, names one person per line and carries a number every team can check on Monday. Today you build that page, and you get three teams to sign it.

## Why one page beats the deck

Phase one of [GTM in a box](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-in-a-box) ends with a single deliverable, the one page GTM plan with owners and targets. Everything before it is input. The ICP from closed won data, the pipeline and channel audit, the competitor and pricing scan. This page is the output, and it is the thing your team works from for the next 60 days.

One page is not a design choice. It is a forcing function. When space runs out, you have to pick. Three segments instead of seven. Two channels per motion instead of everything you tried last year. One name in the owner column instead of "marketing team". The deck lets you avoid those choices. The page does not.

Here are the eight boxes.

1. Target. The revenue number and the pipeline it needs.
2. Segments. Who you sell to first, ranked, with who you will not chase.
3. Motions. Outbound, partners, inbound, and the share of pipeline each owes.
4. Channels. The specific places each motion runs.
5. Budget split. Money follows pipeline share, not habit.
6. Owners. One human per row.
7. Weekly metrics. Six numbers, reviewed every week.
8. Risks. Three things that could break the plan, each with a trigger.

Emily Kramer makes a sharp point in the [MKT1 guide to annual marketing planning](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/annual-marketing-planning): "A couple KPIs (key performance indicators) is not a marketing plan." She splits goals into KPIs, projects and ops work, and she asks you to write down non goals too. That is the same idea in a different shape. A target with no motions, owners or risks attached is a wish with a spreadsheet.

The last piece is the one most teams skip. Signatures. A plan that sales, marketing and partners never agreed to in the same room becomes three private plans by week three. Sales chases its own list. Marketing reports its own number. Partners register deals nobody else knew about. The signing meeting is cheap insurance against all of that.

The rest of this issue gives you the method for each box, the fill in the blanks template, a worked example and the exact meeting that gets the page signed.

## Fill the boxes in this order

Order matters, because each box limits the next one.

1. Target. Start with the revenue number the board signed for the quarter. Divide it by your real win rate from the last two quarters. That gives you pipeline needed. If you want the full math, the [$2M pipeline on a $100K budget](https://syedcreates.com/2m-pipeline-100k-budget) issue walks through it in five lines.
2. Segments. Pick at most three, ranked. Each one gets a one line description and a disqualifier. Add a non goals line for the segment you are deliberately ignoring this quarter. Writing it down stops the debate every time a shiny lead appears.
3. Motions. Assign a pipeline share to each motion, in percentages that add up to 100. This is where the real argument happens, so have it here, on paper, before anyone spends.
4. Channels. List no more than two channels per motion. If outbound runs on email and calls, LinkedIn waits. You can add it next quarter if the first two convert.
5. Budget split. Multiply each motion's opportunity count by its cost per opportunity. The result tells you the split. Keep about 10% as a test reserve so you can try one new thing without stealing from what works.
6. Owners. One person per row. The owner is whoever reports that number in the weekly review. A team is not an owner.
7. Weekly metrics. Pick three leading numbers, such as accounts touched, meetings booked and partner deals registered. Pick three lagging numbers, such as opportunities created, pipeline value and win rate. Six total.
8. Risks. Write the top three. Each needs a trigger date and a response. "If outbound books fewer than 10 meetings by week four, we move half the outbound budget to partners."

## Get sales, marketing and partners to sign it

Signing is a process, not a moment. Here is the one I recommend.

1. Draft it alone. One person writes the first version. Committees write decks.
2. Run three 1:1s before any group meeting. Sit with the sales lead, the marketing lead and the partner lead for 30 minutes each. Each person edits their own row and pushes back on the target in private, where it is safe.
3. Reconcile the two forecasts. Kramer describes a top down forecast that finance and sales build back from the revenue target, and a bottom up forecast that marketing builds from funnel volume and conversion. When they disagree, she recommends a cross functional conversation. Do that in the 1:1s. Close the gap in one of three ways. Raise budget, lower the target, or name the gap as a stretch goal with an owner.
4. Hold a 45 minute signing meeting. The page goes on screen. Each owner reads their row aloud and says the number they own. Then everyone initials and dates the bottom of the page. It feels formal. That is the point.
5. Review it weekly. The page becomes the agenda for the weekly pipeline review with sales. Every GTM in a box project already runs a weekly 45 minute working session, and this page is what we open first.
6. Re sign when a risk fires. If a trigger hits, update the page and get initials again. Plans change. Silent changes are what break trust.

## The one page GTM plan template

Copy this into a doc and fill the blanks. If it spills onto a second page, cut until it does not.

```

1. TARGET
   New revenue this quarter ______   Win rate ______
   Pipeline needed ______ (revenue divided by win rate)
   Average deal size ______   Opportunities needed ______

2. SEGMENTS (ranked, max 3)
   A ______  Disqualifier ______
   B ______  Disqualifier ______
   C ______  Disqualifier ______
   Not this quarter ______

3. MOTIONS AND PIPELINE SHARE (adds up to 100%)
   Outbound ______%   Partners ______%   Inbound ______%

4. CHANNELS (max 2 per motion)
   Outbound ______ / ______
   Partners ______ / ______
   Inbound  ______ / ______

   Outbound ______  Partners ______  Inbound ______  Test reserve ______

   Leading   ______ ______ ______
```

## Worked example with illustrative numbers

Every number below is made up to show the shape. Picture a Series A cloud security startup planning one quarter.

- Target. $600K of new revenue at a 20% win rate needs $3M of pipeline. At a $30K average deal, that is 100 opportunities.
- Segments. A is mid market fintech running Kubernetes. B is SaaS companies facing their first SOC 2 audit. C is regional banks buying through partners. Not this quarter, large enterprises with year long procurement.
- Motions. Outbound 40%, partners 25%, inbound 35%. That is 40, 25 and 35 opportunities.
- Channels. Outbound on email and calls. Partners through two distributors and one MSSP. Inbound through use case pages and webinars.
- Budget split. With $25K for the quarter, outbound data and tools take $6K, partner enablement and joint events take $7K, inbound content and webinars take $9.5K, and $2.5K sits in the test reserve.
- Weekly metrics. Leading targets are 60 accounts touched, 8 meetings booked and 2 partner deals registered. Lagging targets are about 8 opportunities created, $230K of pipeline and win rate tracked monthly.
- Risks. If partner registrations are under 5 by week five, the partner owner runs a joint account mapping session with each distributor. If inbound meetings drop for two weeks in a row, the inbound owner pauses new content and fixes conversion on the top three pages.

Not sure your inputs are solid enough to plan on? Take the two minute [GTM health check](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-health-check) first. If you cannot say which channel sourced each deal last quarter, fix that before you set shares.

## Common mistakes

- Five segments. If everything is a priority, sales picks whatever they like, and the plan stops mattering.
- Team names in the owner column. "Demand gen" cannot be asked a question on Monday. A person can.
- Budget split by last year's habit. Money should follow the pipeline share you just agreed, not the events calendar you inherited.
- Only lagging metrics. By the time pipeline value drops, the quarter is half gone. Leading numbers warn you in week two.
- Risks with no trigger. "Partners may be slow" is a worry. "Under 5 registrations by week five" is a plan.
- Signing it and filing it. The page earns its keep only if it opens every weekly review. The [GTM case study](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-case-study) shows what happens when every motion reports into pipeline every week.

## What to do today

1. Write your target box. Revenue, win rate, pipeline needed and opportunities needed. Ten minutes.
2. Rank your segments and write one non goal.
3. Set pipeline shares for each motion and put one name next to each.
4. Book the three 1:1s with your sales, marketing and partner leads for this week.
5. Put the signing meeting on the calendar before Friday.
