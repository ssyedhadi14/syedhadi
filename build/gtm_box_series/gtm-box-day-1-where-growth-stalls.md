Most stuck B2B startups do not have a demand problem. They have a wiring problem. Deals still route through the founder, every channel reports to its own number, partners sign and go quiet, and nobody can say which dollar moved which deal.

This series fixes the wiring in 10 issues. One module a day, each one lifted from the [GTM in a box](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-in-a-box) roadmap I run with B2B SaaS and security teams. Today is free, start to finish. Read it, score yourself, and you will know exactly which days matter most for you.

## Four places growth stalls

These are the four patterns on the GTM in a box page. Most stuck teams recognise at least one within a minute of reading the list.

### 1. Founder led sales

Every deal still needs the founder in the room. That works for the first ten customers. After that, the founder becomes the bottleneck, and the pipeline grows only as fast as one calendar.

Seth DeHart's [founder led sales framework in Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/founder-led-sales-framework) maps six phases, from early product market fit through hiring a sales leader. His sharpest point is that the founder never fully leaves revenue. The role changes, but one founder stays the steward of it, in his words, "Forever." So the fix is not getting the founder out. The fix is writing down what the founder knows, so other people can sell it.

### 2. Channels in silos

Inbound, outbound and partners report to different numbers. Marketing celebrates MQLs, sales celebrates meetings, partnerships celebrates signed agreements. Each team looks busy. The revenue line stays flat.

### 3. Partners who sign, not sell

Agreements are signed. Joint pipeline is zero. This one hurts the most because it looks like progress on a slide.

Jay McBain, then chief analyst at Canalys, has argued that the average buyer works with about seven partners in a single buying process, according to [this Channel Futures summary of his ecosystem post](https://www.channelfutures.com/channel-sales-marketing/jay-mcbain-shares-key-steps-for-building-ecosystems). If partners already sit inside your buyer's decision, a logo on your partner page is not the goal. Shared accounts, shared pipeline and a monthly review are.

### 4. No idea what moves deals

Spend goes out, and nobody can trace it to revenue. When the budget review comes, the loudest channel wins instead of the one that converts.

I lived this one. At AccuKnox, pipeline used to depend on events and outbound, and nobody could trace which channel produced which deal. The [GTM case study](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-case-study) shows what changed once tracking came first: website sessions grew 240% in 90 days, and the engine now supports $2M in influenced pipeline a year on a $100K a year marketing budget. Tracking came first, content second, scale third. The series follows the same order.

## Score yourself in five minutes

Answer each question honestly. Give yourself 2 points for Yes, 1 for Partly and 0 for No.

| No. | Question | Stall it tests | Your points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Can someone other than the founder run a discovery call and close a deal? | Founder led sales | |
| 2 | Is your sales process written down, with stages and exit criteria? | Founder led sales | |
| 3 | Do you have a written ICP built from closed won deals, with disqualifiers? | Founder led sales | |
| 4 | Do marketing, sales and partners report into one pipeline number? | Channels in silos | |
| 5 | Do sales and marketing review pipeline together every week? | Channels in silos | |
| 6 | Do outbound and partners work the same target account list? | Channels in silos | |
| 7 | Did at least one partner source a qualified deal in the last 90 days? | Partners who sign | |
| 8 | Do you run a joint pipeline review with your top partners every month? | Partners who sign | |
| 9 | Do you know which channel sourced each deal last quarter? | What moves deals | |
| 10 | Do you review win and loss reasons every month and change spend because of them? | What moves deals | |

Add up your points and multiply by 5 to get a score out of 100.

- 0 to 40, stalled. Start with Days 2 and 3. You need data before you need tactics.
- 45 to 70, leaking. Your foundation exists, but channels do not connect. Days 5, 6 and 9 will pay off fastest.
- 75 to 100, ready to scale. Skip to the build. Days 7, 8 and 10 are where your next jump hides.

Look at which stall your lowest answers cluster under. That is your first fix. If you want a second opinion, the interactive [GTM health check](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-health-check) uses the same scoring math and gives you your top three fixes.

## The 3 phase roadmap

The full GTM in a box engagement runs 90 days in three phases. This series compresses each phase into issues you can work through in a day each.

![GTM in a Box 10 day roadmap across three phases, diagnose and decide, build and launch, run and scale](/img/blog/gtm-box-day-1-where-growth-stalls-1.png)

Phase 1, diagnose and decide. Find out who actually buys, where the pipeline leaks and how you compare with competitors. Then make choices and write them on one page.

Phase 2, build and launch. Turn those choices into an outbound motion, a partner program, a launch plan and the attribution that ties it all to pipeline.

Phase 3, run and scale. Run weekly pipeline reviews, double down on the two channels that convert and hand the playbook to your team so it works without you.

## What the next 9 issues deliver

1. Day 2, your ICP from closed won data. Pull won and lost deals, find the patterns and write an ICP and persona sheet based on evidence, not opinions.
2. Day 3, pipeline, win loss and channel audit. One afternoon to see where deals stall, why you lose and which channels actually source revenue.
3. Day 4, competitor and pricing scan. Map the alternatives buyers compare you to and check whether your pricing signals the value you claim.
4. Day 5, the one page GTM plan. Segments, motions, owners and targets on a single page the whole company can read.
5. Day 6, the outbound motion. Lists, sequences and talk tracks pointed at your best fit accounts.
6. Day 7, the partner program. Tiers, deal registration and your first partners mapped to shared accounts.
7. Day 8, the launch plan. Webinars, events and content tied to a pipeline target, not a vanity metric.
8. Day 9, attribution and the pipeline dashboard. Every channel traced to pipeline, in one view your team checks weekly.
9. Day 10, run and scale. The weekly review rhythm and the team playbook you hand over.

By Day 10 you hold the same six assets clients keep after a GTM in a box project: a GTM plan, an ICP and persona sheet, outbound sequences, a partner program kit, a pipeline dashboard and a team playbook.
