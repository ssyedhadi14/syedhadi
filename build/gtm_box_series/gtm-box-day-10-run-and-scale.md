Nine days of building means nothing if it stops the week the builder leaves. Most GTM engines do not break. They just quietly stop being run. Today is about the rhythm that keeps yours alive, and the playbook that lets your team run it without you.

## Rhythm beats heroics

Phase three of [GTM in a box](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-in-a-box) has four lines. Weekly pipeline reviews with sales. Double down on the two channels that convert. Joint pipeline reviews with partners. Operating playbook handed to your team. None of that is glamorous. All of it is where the money shows up.

At AccuKnox, phase three of our own run looked the same. We held weekly pipeline reviews with sales, doubled down on the channels that converted, and built systems that removed manual work, such as a quote generator that cut quotes from days to minutes. You can see the full arc on the [GTM case study](https://syedcreates.com/gtm-case-study). The lesson we wrote there still holds. GTM works when every motion reports into pipeline.

## Three cadences, not thirty meetings

You need exactly three recurring conversations.

- Weekly, with sales. Pipeline, pacing and next steps on live deals. Thirty minutes.
- Biweekly or monthly, with each active partner. Joint pipeline on shared accounts. Forty five minutes.
- Monthly, with leadership. Channel spend against cost per opportunity. One decision minimum.

Quarterly, you revisit the ICP and positioning, or sooner after a major product change. Everything else is optional. If a meeting does not change a deal, a dollar or a priority, cancel it.

## Why only two channels

The hardest call in phase three is what to stop doing. Brian Balfour argues that teams should focus on one or two channels at a time rather than testing a bunch, because growth usually comes from a single channel the product has been shaped to fit. He cites Peter Thiel's line that "distribution follows the power law" in his essay on [product channel fit](https://brianbalfour.com/essays/product-channel-fit-for-growth). Spreading thin feels safe. It is actually the riskiest thing a small team can do.

The rest of this issue gives you the agenda, the two channel scorecard and the table of contents for the playbook you hand over.

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the weekly review slot. Same day, same time, same owner. Monday or Tuesday works best so actions land inside the week.
2. Send the dashboard 24 hours before. The five charts from Day 9, plus a short written note on what moved and why.
3. Run the agenda below. Every metric has an owner who explains it. Questions nobody can answer get logged and answered within a day.
4. Run joint reviews with partners on shared accounts only. Bring the account map, not your full pipeline.
5. Score channels monthly. Pick the two with the best mix of pipeline, cost per opportunity and win rate. Cut or pause the rest.
6. Write the playbook as you go. Every decision rule you repeat twice goes in the doc.

The Amazon weekly business review is a useful model here. A former Amazon employee, writing a guest post in the [Data Analysis Journal](https://dataanalysis.substack.com/p/how-amazon-runs-a-weekly-business-review), describes reviews built on a short written document instead of slides. Everyone reads it in the first ten minutes, each metric has a named owner, and unanswered questions get an email reply within a day. The part that stuck with me was the ban on slides and vague summaries. Owners write a narrative that explains why a metric moved. Steal the habit, skip the bureaucracy.

## Template one, the weekly pipeline review agenda

Thirty minutes. Paste this into a recurring invite.

| Minutes | Item | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Silent read of the written note and dashboard | Everyone | Questions noted |
| 5 to 10 | Pacing vs quarter target, green, amber or red | Sales lead | One line status |
| 10 to 18 | New opportunities this week, source and self reported source checked | Marketing and SDR lead | Fields fixed live |
| 18 to 24 | Deals stuck more than 14 days in one stage | Account executives | Next step and date per deal |
| 24 to 28 | Channel signal, what moved and why | Marketing | Keep, test or cut |
| 28 to 30 | Actions with owners and dates | Meeting owner | Written in notes, shared same day |

For partner reviews, swap rows three and four for shared account updates, deal registration status and the next joint activity. If you ran Day 7, you already have the partner side mapped. The [partner programs post](https://syedcreates.com/partner-programs-that-ship-pipeline) covers the partner cadence, from weekly pipeline calls to monthly business reviews.

## Template two, the two channel scorecard

Score each active channel from 1 to 5 on four lines. Add them up. Keep the top two.

- Pipeline sourced last 90 days
- Cost per opportunity, lower scores higher
- Win rate on sourced opportunities
- Fit with your ICP, measured by share of target accounts reached

## Template three, the team playbook table of contents

This is what you hand over. Each section is one to two pages, written for the person who joins next month.

1. The 90 day GTM plan, with owners and targets
2. ICP and persona sheet, including disqualifiers
3. Positioning and the one line pitch every rep uses
4. Outbound sequences, lists and talk tracks
5. Partner program kit, tiers, deal registration and rules of engagement
6. Launch plan template for webinars, events and content
7. Attribution rules, UTM list and CRM field definitions
8. Pipeline dashboard, chart by chart, with owners
9. Meeting rhythm, agendas and who attends
10. Decision rules, when to cut a channel, when to add one, when to escalate a deal

Those first eight map straight to what you keep after day 90 in GTM in a box. The 90 day GTM plan, ICP and persona sheet, outbound sequences, partner program kit, pipeline dashboard and team playbook.

## Worked example

Illustrative numbers only, not from any real company.

- Channel scores after month two. Outbound 16, partners 14, webinars 13, paid social 8, content syndication 7.
- Keep outbound and partners. Webinars stay as a supporting play for partner accounts. Pause paid social and syndication.
- Freed budget, say $6K a month, moves to a second SDR list and one joint partner event.
- Four weeks later, chart five on the dashboard moves from amber to green. The decision rule goes into section 10 of the playbook.

## Common mistakes

- Reviewing pipeline without the dashboard sent in advance. The meeting turns into a status read out.
- Letting partner reviews cover every account. Stick to shared accounts or partners tune out.
- Doubling down on four channels. That is not doubling down.
- Writing the playbook in the last week. Write it while you build, or it reads like a eulogy.
- Never cutting a channel. A channel you cannot defend in the monthly review should not survive the quarter.
